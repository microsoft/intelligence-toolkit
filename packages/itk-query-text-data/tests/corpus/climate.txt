Climate change is reshaping coastal cities. Sea level rise threatens coastal cities with flooding, and extreme weather events are becoming more frequent. Scientists link climate change to carbon emissions from fossil fuels.

Carbon emissions continue to grow in many regions. Reducing carbon emissions requires renewable energy investment, and renewable energy now supplies a growing share of electricity. Wind turbines and solar panels are the main sources of renewable energy.

Heat waves are a direct consequence of climate change. Heat waves stress power grids and raise energy prices. Extreme weather also damages infrastructure in coastal cities, increasing insurance costs.

Sea level rise forces coastal cities to build flood defenses. Flood defenses are expensive, and insurance costs rise as extreme weather intensifies. Climate change adaptation plans now include flood defenses and early warning systems.
